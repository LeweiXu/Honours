This study investigates the stability and performance of reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning for large language models (LLMs) within the DeepRetrieval framework for structured query generation. Using Qwen2-0.5B-Instruct and LLaMA-3.2-3B-Instruct as policy models, a series of controlled experiments explored how learning rate, KL penalty, PPO epochs, and algorithmic design (PPO vs. GRPO) affect training dynamics. Results show that learning rate overwhelmingly governs stability, while reward scaling and secondary hyperparameters exert limited influence. Larger models exhibited smoother optimization, higher baseline rewards, and more stable convergence, but learning efficiency remained constrained by sparse reward signals. Switching from PPO to GRPO yielded substantial improvements in both stability and efficiency, particularly in learning structured query formats, though recall-based optimization remained challenging. These findings highlight the delicate balance between stability and learning speed in RL fine-tuning, suggesting that future work should focus on reward design and systematic exploration of model scaling effects to improve training reliability and retrieval performance. The full implementation and training pipeline are publicly available for reproducibility at \url{https://github.com/dyts11/deepretrieval}.
