Agentic Methods

Agentic methods put one or more LLM controllers in charge: either iterating evidence acquisition under inference-time control, or coordinating across specialised roles.

  • ReAct and tool-augmented reasoning. A single controller interleaves thought, action, and observation. Actions are document-side tool calls (retrievers, parsers, OCR engines) or structured navigation over a pre-built document representation.
  • Multi-agent orchestration. Modality-specialised decomposition gives text and visual evidence separate controllers plus a synthesis controller. Role-specialised decomposition splits planning, execution, verification, and synthesis.
  • What the adaptive trajectory buys. Compute scales with question difficulty rather than document length, the single-step recall ceiling of retrieval-augmented pipelines is lifted, and the action sequence is an inspectable audit trail.

The cost is controllability: inference cost is hard to bound, and a controller can commit early to wrong evidence and continue down the wrong trajectory.
