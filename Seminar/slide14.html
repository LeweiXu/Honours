Agentic Methods

An agentic system decides what to inspect next as it goes, rather than executing a plan fixed in advance. Each observation feeds the next decision.

  • It can go back for more. Search again with a refined query, follow a cross-reference to another section, or request a closer look at a page it has already seen.
  • It can divide the work. Some systems split the roles: a reader that gathers, a planner that decides, a checker that decides whether the answer is good enough.
  • Compute follows difficulty. An easy question stops early; a hard one keeps going. The trajectory also becomes an audit trail of what the system actually looked at.

The trade-off is time, and more opportunities for a bad decision to lead the system astray. It also still inherits the recall ceiling of every tool it calls.
