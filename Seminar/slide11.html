Reasoning Strategies

Reasoning acts over a single fixed evidence buffer assembled before the call. The step itself acquires nothing new, so its contribution lies in how faithfully the delivered evidence is used, not in recovering evidence the buffer omits.

Inference-time

Sequential. Chain-of-thought elicits intermediate reasoning before the answer, which binds evidence from different pages step by step, decomposes multi-hop questions into single-hop ones, and externalises the trace for verification. Self-reflection extends the same thread with iterative revision against the buffer, optionally under a verifier.

Parallel. Self-consistency samples several chains and takes the majority, or has an LLM adjudicate between reasoning paths. Tree-of-thoughts explores branches with backtracking, but has seen limited uptake, since in MP-VRDU evidence sparsity rather than search branching is the dominant difficulty.

Trained

Trace imitation. Supervise the backbone on demonstration chains wrapped in fixed schema tags such as <think>, <evidence>, and <answer>, refined with DPO to prefer faithful chains over hallucinated shortcuts.

Reward-driven. Optimise the reasoning policy with GRPO against a composite reward of answer accuracy, evidence-page recall, and format compliance. Usually warm-started from imitation rather than learned from scratch, so the two are sequential rather than alternative.

Uncertainty calibration. Train an aggregation head that selects across candidate outputs by calibrated uncertainty, giving a learned self-consistency signal without sampling several chains at test time.

All of it is independent of architectural family, and all of it is bounded the same way: missing evidence cannot be reasoned back into existence.
