Cross-Page Structure Modality

A long document is more than a stack of independent pages. Its structure is what turns isolated page observations into document evidence.

  • Hierarchy and reading order. Sections, lists, and captions tell us how the parts fit together, and the same evidence can mean different things depending on which section it sits under.
  • Cross-page references. A sentence on one page points to a figure on another. Resolving “see Figure 3” to its referent is not something similarity scoring can do.
  • Spanning elements. Tables and arguments continue across page boundaries, so the useful unit is larger than a page but smaller than the document.

Systems recover this by building outlines, trees, or graphs over the document. Errors in that structure then mislead every step that follows.
