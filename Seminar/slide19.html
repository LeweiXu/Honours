Retrieval and Navigation

Retrieval narrows a long document to compact evidence units (chunks, pages, or graph nodes) for downstream reasoning.

  • Similarity-based retrieval. Scores each evidence unit independently against the query and returns the top-ranked subset. Sparse lexical signals such as BM25 are the baseline; dense retrieval uses vision-language late-interaction encoders such as ColPali and ColQwen, often with an LLM reranking stage after.
  • Relation-aware retrieval. Retrieves along explicit document structure. Tree-based variants follow section hierarchies and outlines, so chunks keep their parent context; graph-based variants traverse typed edges between headings, entities, and cross-references.
  • The trade-off. Independent scoring handles paraphrase and cross-modal queries, but cannot follow cross-page references or multi-hop dependencies. Relation-aware methods can, at the cost of parser dependence and traversal cost.

Evidence missed or poorly ranked at retrieval is unrecoverable at generation. What is retrieved still has to be reasoned over.
