Retrieval and Navigation

When a question arrives, retrieval narrows the document to a smaller set of pages or passages, so the reasoner never has to process the whole thing.

  • Four ways to search. By words, with a sparse lexical scorer. By learned similarity over text embeddings. By image, with late-interaction encoders that score the question against the rendered page directly. Or by structure, walking a tree or graph built over the document.
  • Structure is what similarity cannot do. Scoring passages independently handles paraphrase well, but it cannot follow a cross-reference or a dependency that no single passage carries.
  • The main risk is omission. If a page the answer requires never reaches the reasoner, nothing downstream can use what was on it.

Finding the evidence is only half the task. The model still has to reason with it.
